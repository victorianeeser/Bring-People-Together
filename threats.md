# Threats

Our scrapbook is a friend group's shared book for the year: the bucket list on page 1, and a spread for every plan they actually do. Anything a group makes together can also be used to hurt, embarrass or leave out someone in it. These are the ways that could happen in our app, and what we'd do about each one.

## Leaving people out

**Plans you weren't part of are right there in the book.**
A group does something without you, and now there's a whole spread about it in a book you're in.
- Every plan on the bucket list is visible to everyone in the book, and anyone can tap "I'm in." There are no hidden plans inside a book.
- A spread for a plan you missed still lets you add a comment or a sticker, so you're part of the page, not just looking at it.

**The "I'm in" list shows who isn't going.**
If five friends say "I'm in" and one doesn't, it's easy to read that as a snub.
- Show who's in. Never show who said no, who saw it, or who hasn't answered.

**Getting removed from the book.**
Being kicked out of the group's book feels public and final.
- No announcement to the group. The removed person gets a private, neutral message ("You're no longer in Roommates 2026") and can keep a copy of their own photos.

**A second book without you.**
Part of the group starts "the real book" and leaves someone out.
- We can't stop people from making a new book, but the app never shows which books other people are in or counts "mutual books."

**The class demo book.**
Joining by QR code means anyone in the room can get in, including people who want to mess with it.
- The demo QR code expires after the demo, and the person running it can remove anyone.

## Bullying and embarrassment

**Unflattering or private photos.**
Someone posts a photo of you that you'd never want the group to see.
- "This is me. Hide it.": anyone can hide a photo of themselves for the whole group with one tap, without asking the person who posted it.

**Mean captions and stickers.**
A caption that's an inside joke at your expense, or a sticker drawn over your face.
- Every caption and sticker shows who added it, in their color. Nothing is anonymous.
- The person in the photo can remove anything added on top of it.
- Anyone can report it.

**Erasing someone from the year.**
Someone deletes or covers up what another person added.
- You can only edit or delete your own things. The book owner can undo changes.

**Piling on.**
A spread turns into a pile of comments aimed at one person.
- Rate limits on comments and stickers per person per spread. Reporting hides the comment for the reporter right away and for everyone after 2 reports.

## Pressure and comparison

**Keeping score.**
Each friend's color makes it easy to see who added the most and who added nothing.
- No counts, no leaderboards, no "most active." Colors show who added something, not how much.

**Guilt reminders.**
"You haven't added anything to Lake Day yet" can feel like being called out.
- At most one gentle reminder per plan, sent only to the person, and no streaks.

**Plans that cost money.**
A bucket list full of concerts and trips leaves out the friend who can't afford them.
- Plans can be tagged "free," and "Can't decide?" can pick only from free plans.

**Pressure to do risky things.**
A plan on the list that someone feels pushed into.
- Anyone can report a plan, and saying "I'm in" is always optional.

## Privacy and control

**Location in photos.**
Photo files can carry the exact place they were taken, like someone's dorm.
- Remove location data from every photo when it's uploaded.

**Sharing outside the group.**
A page gets exported or set as a wallpaper and sent to people who weren't in the book.
- Exports are off unless the group turns them on. We can't block screenshots, so the privacy note says so honestly.

**Feeling watched.**
Live cursors and colors show when you're on the page.
- Blocking someone hides your presence from them.

## Painful memories

**"One year ago today" after a falling out.**
The app brings back photos with an ex, a friend you lost, or someone who passed away.
- Turn the feature off, or hide memories with a specific person. People you've blocked or who left the book never show up in memories.

**An ex who's still in the group.**
After a breakup, one of them posts couple photos, adds plans about the relationship, or uses the book to keep tabs on the other.
- Everything above applies: hide photos of yourself, report, block, and remove members.
- Intimate photos are removed for everyone on the first report and sent to us. The reporter gets links to [Take It Down](https://takeitdown.ncmec.org/) (under 18) and [StopNCII](https://stopncii.org/) (18 and over).

## The least we'd build to keep people safe

1. Books are private and invite-only, with no public pages and no search.
2. Invite links expire after 7 days, and removing someone resets the link.
3. "This is me. Hide it." on any photo.
4. You can only edit or delete your own things. The owner can undo.
5. Report, which hides content for you right away and for everyone after 2 reports (1 for intimate photos).
6. Block, which hides their content and presence from you and stops them adding you to books.
7. No counts or rankings, and no "who said no" or "seen" lists.
8. Leave a book anytime and take your photos with you.
9. Remove location data from photos.
10. Rate limits on uploads, comments, invites and reminders.

**How we'd test it:** in our next round of user testing, ask people to hide a photo of themselves, report a caption and leave a book, and time each one. If it takes more than about 10 seconds or they need help, it's too hard to find.
