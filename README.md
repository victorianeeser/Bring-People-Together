# Bring-People-Together

## Our idea: a shared scrapbook for your friend group

**A book your friend group makes together, one book per year.** Page 1 of the book is the group's bucket list, the things you keep saying "we should do." When the group actually does one, it turns into a scrapbook spread with everyone's photos, notes and stickers. The bucket list works like a table of contents: tap a finished plan and it jumps to its spread.

### How it works
1. **Start a book.** Someone makes a book for the group (like "Roommates 2026") and invites friends with a link or QR code.
2. **Make the list.** Everyone adds plans to page 1, sorted by season (summer, fall, winter, spring, someday). Friends tap "I'm in" on the ones they want to do.
3. **Do the plan.** When the group does something, someone checks it off.
4. **Make the spread together.** Everyone adds photos, text and stickers to that plan's page at the same time. Each friend has their own color, so you can see who's adding what.
5. **Look back.** Flip through the book, or jump to any spread from the list. Each year is its own book, so older years sit on a shelf.

### Why
In our interviews, nobody said they didn't want to hang out. Plans fall apart because of schedules, last-minute homework and work shifts, and group chats that go quiet. A shared list shows what the group said it would do, and the book shows that you actually did it. People told us it's the friends that make something worth showing up for, so every page shows who was there.

### Who it's for
Friend groups first, like college roommates and friends from a team or club. Families and couples could use it later.

### Ideas we want to try
- **Fill in the year:** a group that starts in November can backfill the earlier part of 2026 with photos they already have.
- **Can't decide?** Randomly pick a plan from the list.
- **Themes:** a few page styles to start from, which you can change page by page.
- **One year ago today:** reopen what the group did last year.
- **Class demo:** for the final presentation, make a book for our class that classmates join with a QR code.

## Who we're designing for, and why

We're designing for **friend groups who care about each other but keep losing the chance to spend time together**, starting with college students like roommates and friends from a team or club. Between the three of us, we interviewed college students, grad students, young workers, a mom of two and a high school student. Nobody said they didn't want to hang out. The problem was always getting it to happen: schedules that never line up, homework or work shifts that cancel plans last minute, and group chats where people read the message and never answer. They already have a running list of "we should do this" ideas, and a camera roll full of photos from the times they did. But nothing ties the two together, so plans get forgotten and memories get buried.

That's why we're making a **shared scrapbook book for each year**. The bucket list on page 1 keeps the group's plans in one place where everyone can say "I'm in." Every finished plan becomes a page the whole group makes together, so the book fills up as the year goes on. People told us it's the friends that make something worth showing up for, so every page shows who was there. Victoria tested an early version with 5 people, and their feedback shaped it: a simple checklist by season instead of a timeline, separate groups, more text and sticker options, and a way to invite friends. Families and couples could use the same book later, but friend groups come first.
