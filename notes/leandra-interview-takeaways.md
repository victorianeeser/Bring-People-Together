# 11/3 | Interview #1 Takeaways

📄 **Full transcript:** [leandra-interview-1-transcript.pdf](transcripts/leandra-interview-1-transcript.pdf)

**Who:** Grad student in a Doctor of Physical Therapy program at Creighton (same school as undergrad, class of ~85). Just started serving at Olive Garden.
**Topic:** How they socialize day to day and what gets in the way of staying connected.

## About them
- Very involved: geriatric PT org, PTLG (community service), student ambassador, geriatric research (joint degeneration), two journal clubs (favorite is pelvic health), and a new job.
- They describe themselves as *chaotic / a little scatterbrained*. Friends would say *empathetic, hardworking, energetic*.

## Routine
- Wakes up around 5am, then works out or gets extra work done.
- Class runs 8–12 or 8–5. Clubs around noon, then work, more clubs, or group projects.
- Studies 3–4 hrs a day, usually including a 2–3 hr "locked in" session at night. At least one test a week.
- Wants to pace studying but doesn't plan ahead, so it often turns into cramming.
- Weekends: one "reset" day (cleaning, meal prep, lighter studying). The rest is mostly studying, plus PT class socials (volleyball, kickball) planned by student government.

## How they study / collaborate
- Doesn't need anyone around to stay accountable.
- **Solo first, group second:** they learn the material alone, then review with others before big exams by teaching each other.
- Gets stressed if someone asks a question before they've learned the material.
- By the end of the day they're "done with people."
- Group projects are mostly in person, using PowerPoint and Google Docs, plus Teams for journal club. Collaboration feels **manual** ("just put it in the document").

## How they stay connected
- **Piggybacks on things they already do:** fits friends into lunch/dinner, walks (loves the outdoors), coffee shops, the free art museum, the ice cream place near their apartment.
- **Hosts at home a lot.** Friends have their location and are welcome to drop by even while they're working (Emma does this often, Izzy texts when he's in town).
- **Type B unless they're the one planning:** if they plan a hangout it's very structured, but they rarely start spontaneous plans. They say yes about 90% of the time when someone else does.
- Sees 1–3 friends per weekend and is "on round two" of catching up with undergrad friends. Scheduling all of that is hard.
- **Channels:** texting first. Instagram reels are a "love language" with some friends. They email TikToks through school Outlook because TikTok is only on their computer (to avoid distraction). They rarely check IG DMs.
- Sends "this reminded me of you" messages and check-ins.

## Pain points
- **Replying takes energy:** they won't answer a text until they can match the other person's energy. Replies can take up to 5 days, and friends say they're bad at texting.
- **Authenticity worry:** a low-energy reply feels like not caring, and a forced one feels fake, so they wait (and sometimes forget).
- They've told friends to react to (*emphasize*) their own message as a reminder if a reply is late.
- **Social energy drain:** even with friends they love, it takes effort, except with one or two long-term friends where there's a "mutual understanding."
- **Overthinking afterward:** "What if I said something wrong?"
- **Scheduling overload:** several friends formally asking for time each weekend, on top of exams, labs, and work.
- (Side note from me: an automated "can't talk right now" reply felt too much like an email.)

## Design opportunities for our platform
- **Low-pressure presence > conversation:** they like friends being around while they work (drop-ins, shared location). A "co-working / body-doubling" space where people are just present could fit.
- **Ways to respond that don't need full energy:** quick reactions, "saw this, will reply later" states, or showing you care without a full reply.
- **Sharing content is connection:** reels and TikToks are how they keep in touch, so make sharing quick and fun.
- **Support solo-then-group:** private space to prep, then a shared space to review and teach each other.
- **Support both structured and spontaneous:** easy invites/drop-ins for spontaneous people, scheduling for planners.
- **Fit into existing routines** (meals, study blocks) instead of adding another thing to schedule.
- Current group tools (Docs, PPT, Teams) work but feel manual, which is a gap for real-time collaboration.
