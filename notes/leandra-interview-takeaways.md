# 11/3 | Interview #1 Takeaways

📄 **Full transcript:** [leandra-interview-1-transcript.pdf](transcripts/leandra-interview-1-transcript.pdf)

**Who:** Grad student in a Doctor of Physical Therapy program at Creighton (same school as undergrad, class of ~85). Just started serving at Olive Garden.
**Topic:** How they socialize day to day and what gets in the way of staying connected.

## About them
- Very involved: geriatric PT org, PTLG (community service), student ambassador, geriatric research (joint degeneration), two journal clubs (favorite is pelvic health), and a new job.
- They describe themselves as *chaotic / a little scatterbrained*. Friends would say *empathetic, hardworking, energetic*.

## Routine
- Wakes up around 5am, then works out or gets extra work done.
- Class runs 8–12 or 8–5. Clubs around noon, then work, more clubs, or group projects.
- Studies 3–4 hrs a day, usually including a 2–3 hr "locked in" session at night. At least one test a week.
- Wants to pace studying but doesn't plan ahead, so it often turns into cramming.
- Weekends: one "reset" day (cleaning, meal prep, lighter studying). The rest is mostly studying, plus PT class socials (volleyball, kickball) planned by student government.

## How they study / collaborate
- Doesn't need anyone around to stay accountable.
- **Solo first, group second:** they learn the material alone, then review with others before big exams by teaching each other.
- Gets stressed if someone asks a question before they've learned the material.
- By the end of the day they're "done with people."
- Group projects are mostly in person, using PowerPoint and Google Docs, plus Teams for journal club. Collaboration feels **manual** ("just put it in the document").

## How they stay connected
- **Piggybacks on things they already do:** fits friends into lunch/dinner, walks (loves the outdoors), coffee shops, the free art museum, the ice cream place near their apartment.
- **Hosts at home a lot.** Friends have their location and are welcome to drop by even while they're working (Emma does this often, Izzy texts when he's in town).
- **Type B unless they're the one planning:** if they plan a hangout it's very structured, but they rarely start spontaneous plans. They say yes about 90% of the time when someone else does.
- Sees 1–3 friends per weekend and is "on round two" of catching up with undergrad friends. Scheduling all of that is hard.
- **Channels:** texting first. Instagram reels are a "love language" with some friends. They email TikToks through school Outlook because TikTok is only on their computer (to avoid distraction). They rarely check IG DMs.
- Sends "this reminded me of you" messages and check-ins.

## Pain points
- **Replying takes energy:** they won't answer a text until they can match the other person's energy. Replies can take up to 5 days, and friends say they're bad at texting.
- **Authenticity worry:** a low-energy reply feels like not caring, and a forced one feels fake, so they wait (and sometimes forget).
- They've told friends to react to (*emphasize*) their own message as a reminder if a reply is late.
- **Social energy drain:** even with friends they love, it takes effort, except with one or two long-term friends where there's a "mutual understanding."
- **Overthinking afterward:** "What if I said something wrong?"
- **Scheduling overload:** several friends formally asking for time each weekend, on top of exams, labs, and work.
- (Side note from me: an automated "can't talk right now" reply felt too much like an email.)

## Design opportunities for our platform
- **Low-pressure presence > conversation:** they like friends being around while they work (drop-ins, shared location). A "co-working / body-doubling" space where people are just present could fit.
- **Ways to respond that don't need full energy:** quick reactions, "saw this, will reply later" states, or showing you care without a full reply.
- **Sharing content is connection:** reels and TikToks are how they keep in touch, so make sharing quick and fun.
- **Support solo-then-group:** private space to prep, then a shared space to review and teach each other.
- **Support both structured and spontaneous:** easy invites/drop-ins for spontaneous people, scheduling for planners.
- **Fit into existing routines** (meals, study blocks) instead of adding another thing to schedule.
- Current group tools (Docs, PPT, Teams) work but feel manual, which is a gap for real-time collaboration.

# 11/3 | Interview #2 Takeaways

🔗 **Full transcript:** [TurboScribe transcript (11/3, 9:41 PM)](https://turboscribe.ai/transcript/share/6341068275486139089/MnfMX53q5Enq0f4XmB9mSfCnZxFE386zq19XW5i6xk4/turboscribe-recording-11-3-2026-9-41-39-pm)

**Who:** Works in sales at a sporting goods store (golf, ski/snowboard, baseball). Golfs almost every day, plays games with friends, has a girlfriend he spends most days off with.
**Topic:** Day-to-day routine, how he socializes, and what gets in the way of keeping connections.

## About him
- Describes himself as *funny, charismatic, tall*. He joked through it and found it hard to say how friends see him ("95% of the time I cannot answer this question").
- Very social at work: talks to coworkers and customers all day in long conversations.

## Routine
- Wakes around 8, works from 9:20. Usually skips breakfast, so he's low on energy.
- Work time drags, and time off "speeds up," so he feels like he has no free time and is exhausted when he gets home.
- Days off: almost always with his girlfriend (movies, TV, going out). Otherwise solo or golfing.

## Social pressure & golf
- Sometimes golfs with friends, sometimes alone with random people.
- **Randoms = less pressure.** Strangers hype you up, and losing to them doesn't matter. Losing to a friend is high stakes ("nightmares for a month").
- Golf is a mental game, and friends watching adds a lot of pressure.

## Work conversations
- Talks with customers about their lives (a dad buying a bat for his son's birthday, ski trips abroad).
- Avoids treating it as **transactional**: just be friendly and ask about their morning, even if they're not buying.
- Work tools: **Gmail** for professional/vendor stuff, **Google Chat** for quick messages to coworkers.

## Gaming with friends
- Used to play Counter-Strike (5v5, team-based) but is taking a break because it's too stressful when he doesn't perform well.
- Now prefers **"friend slop" games**, casual chaotic co-op games like Lethal Company and PEAK.
- The vibe depends on who's in the lobby ("if the dude's a chiller, we're just gonna chill").
- **Communication:** someone hops on, friends see they're **online**, click call, and they're in. He has Discord servers but doesn't use them and just uses a group chat plus calls. No real reason besides habit.

## How he connects
- Mainly through **shared interests/hobbies** (golf, snowboarding), not work.
- Keeps connections by "talking to them," but couldn't really explain how beyond that.

## Pain points
- **Doesn't take initiative:** friends (e.g. a high school friend, a golf buddy from work) are always the ones who reach out. He says yes but "never even thought about" asking first.
- **Forgetting** to reach out.
- Puts in effort with his girlfriend but less with friends ("I just don't care as much"). Friendships have lower stakes and expectations.
- Thinks not reaching out could hurt making new connections long-term.

## Feeling "others are here too"
- **YouTube:** comments and view counts make you realize millions of people are doing the same thing on a Thursday night.
- **Live streams (Twitch/Kick):** live view counts and chat. He personally dislikes streams ("a TV show with a thousand episodes" vs. YouTube's highlights) and thinks commenting is pointless, but admits he still spams W's in chat.
- He understands that stream chats can become communities, it's just not for him.

## Design opportunities for our platform
- **Show who's online → one-click join:** his whole gaming flow is seeing a friend online and hopping in. Low-friction presence is the key behavior.
- **Nudge the non-initiators:** reminders, prompts, or "X is free / doing Y, want to join?" so he doesn't have to be the one who plans.
- **Low-stakes play:** he's stressed by competitive pressure with friends. Casual, chaotic, cooperative activities (like friend slop games) may work better than ranked competition.
- **Lightweight participation:** even someone who thinks commenting is "dumb" still spams quick reactions (W's). Fast, low-effort interactions get used.
- **Ambient "people are here" signals** (view counts, live activity) can create a sense of shared space without forcing conversation.
- **Build around shared interests**, since that's where his real connections come from.
- Group chat + calls beat structured servers for small friend groups, so keep it simple and avoid over-organizing.

# 11/3 | Interview #3 Takeaways

🔗 **Full transcript:** [TurboScribe transcript (11/3, 10:26 PM)](https://turboscribe.ai/transcript/share/5557441940328475049/sFIahoF_dKIRJdxICD_eynSIbUj9jc85JjswiPW9MLs/turboscribe-recording-11-3-2026-10-26-05-pm)

**Who:** Senior in undergrad on the biology/pre-optometry track (wants to own her own optometry practice). Works at a food spot near campus, is in Women in Business, and lives with her roommate.
**Topic:** Day-to-day routine, how she socializes, and what gets in the way of connecting.

## About her
- Friends' first impression of her: *"school and grades"* (and "kind of boring"). She agrees, since she'll blow off plans to study and won't hang out until an exam is over.
- Under heavy pressure: undergrad feels like "high school all over again" because she still has to get into grad school. Feels like no one else is on her path.
- Knows she's in a "work now, enjoy later" mode and has been having a bit of a crisis about it, but it's "a problem for later."

## Routine
- Wakes up around 6:30–7 (her roommate's sunrise alarm). Makes breakfast for both of them and they eat together, sometimes outside.
- Normally goes to the gym with her roommate, but during exam weeks she "forfeits everything" to study.
- Classes start around 10–12 and are all **lecture-based**, so she doesn't talk to the people next to her. She only has a friend in 1–2 classes (they picked schedules together).
- After class she eats and goes home, and realized mid-interview she "really doesn't talk to anybody."
- Free time: doom scrolling in bed because she feels she's "earned it." Also likes grocery shopping and cooking.

## Work = most of her socializing
- Works with **just one coworker** per shift. It's an "intimate" setting where you get to know that one person really well.
- Busy rushes mean no talking for an hour or two, and slow periods mean you could "debrief your whole life story."
- Gets to know **regulars** (mostly students), e.g. a regular they nicknamed after his favorite artist in the POS rewards system. She knows what they're up to that week but will "never see them outside of work."
- Likes getting out of her comfort zone talking to strangers.

## How she studies
- Stopped going to **big study groups** because she was never on the same level as others. She wasn't gaining or contributing, and it stressed her out.
- When she did understand something, she'd teach others, but it wasn't **reciprocated**, so it felt like lost time.
- Found a study partner through the campus **tutoring center** who's on the same page, so they learn together, help each other equally, and keep each other accountable.
- Bio classes have basically **no group projects**. The one she had was a slide deck where everyone just did their own part with no real collaboration.

## How she connects
- **Hosting:** early in the semester she and her roommate hosted friends a lot and played deep-conversation card games (We're Not Really Strangers style), just sitting and talking for hours.
- **Snapchat** is the main way her friends stay connected, but she **dislikes communicating on it**. She turned off Snap/IG notifications ~4 years ago, has 3 people pinned, and mostly just watches stories.
- Prefers **iMessage** because she'll actually see it.
- Snapchat stories feel funny and personal (you choose who sees them), while Instagram stories feel more serious/performative.
- **GroupMe** for study groups and clubs ("everybody in college uses GroupMe"). One physics professor uses **Discord** for class communication.

## Pain points
- **No time:** her school schedule crowds out socializing.
- **No natural place to meet people on her path:** lecture classes don't allow peer interaction, and reaching out to strangers outside class takes effort.
- **Hasn't found people she clicks with**, partly because she's too busy to meet many people.
- **Mismatched group dynamics:** groups where people are at different levels feel like a waste of time.
- Notifications are off on social apps, so messages there get missed.

## Design opportunities for our platform
- **Match people at the same level:** she thrives with a partner on the same page. Help people find others at a similar point (same class, same topic, same progress).
- **Make collaboration reciprocal:** show what each person is contributing or struggling with so help goes both ways.
- **Small groups > big groups:** her best interactions are one-on-one or a few people (one coworker, one study buddy, a few friends at home).
- **Fit socializing into what she's already doing** (studying, meals, cooking), since she won't add extra social time during exam weeks.
- **Help people in lecture classes connect:** her classmates are "on the same path" but she has no way to talk to them.
- **Conversation prompts work:** deep-question card games got her friends talking for hours, so prompts or icebreakers could help.
- **Respect notification fatigue:** she turned off social notifications entirely, so anything we build needs to be easy to check and not noisy.
- **Privacy/control over who sees what** made Snapchat stories feel more personal than Instagram.
