# 11/3 | Maya's Interview Notes

Three interviews: Addie, Lanie and Anthony.

📄 **Full transcripts:** [Addie](transcripts/maya-interview-1-addie-transcript.txt) · [Lanie](transcripts/maya-interview-2-lanie-transcript.txt)

## Participant notes

### 1. Addie
- **Splits her time about 50/50 between groups and being alone.** Group time means going out (basketball games, dinners). Time alone is for getting things done.
- **Makes friends through other friends:** random freshman roommates, friends of friends in Greek life, the gym, and "signing up for spontaneous events."
- **A good first meeting is a balanced one:** "zero awkwardness… a two-way conversation."
- **Plans fall through at the last minute.** High school was planned and close-knit. In college, homework or a work shift can kill plans at the last minute.
- **Uses games as social glue:** iMessage Crazy Eights ("you can't cheat on it"), Heads Up with big groups, and solo puzzle games (Candy Crush, Sudoku, crosswords).
- **Talking to strangers online cuts both ways.** Fortnite voice chat and Omegle can be fun. But "you never know what you're gonna get," and as a teen girl she ran into older men who were weird or rude.
- **What she wishes existed:** a way to get everyone replying in the same time frame. People who read the text and don't reply, or say yes and don't show up, "drive me crazy."

### 2. Lanie
- **Her social life comes from one group plus her roommates.** Club soccer (3 practices a week, games, nationals) is her main group. She meets new people through her teammates or her roommates' friends.
- **Joined because a friend pulled her in.** A high school friend talked her into soccer. She'd "never really put myself out there."
- **Stays for the people:** "The girls are what makes it worth showing up," even when they lose. She'd only quit if school got too heavy.
- **Plans fail because schedules don't line up.** A study session fell through because the group couldn't find more than 30 minutes when everyone was free.
- **Uses few apps:** Snapchat and GroupMe, then iMessage and Instagram DMs. Not interested in spending time with strangers online.
- **Silence in a group chat is the main pain point.** Everyone opens a message and nobody answers. Or the chat moves too fast and she's "five chats behind." When people ghost her, she just texts other people instead.

### 3. Anthony
- **Not in any groups yet.** Wants to join AKPsi and the accounting club next semester.
- **Small-world connections:** he met someone new through a friend's boyfriend at a bar and found out they share people they both know.
- **Plans fell through when someone else's priorities won.** An exam study session ended when his study partner went to a sales meeting instead.
- **Old friendships survive without regular contact:** with friends from home, "every time I see him it's like we've been talking for the past year."
- **Plays two ways:** casual mobile games alone (Block Blast, block-shooter puzzles; "multiplayer would be pretty cool") and online multiplayer on Xbox (Rocket League, Rainbow Six Siege, GTA).
- **Playing with strangers is normal for him.** He plays with strangers "all the time," matched in by the system. Gamer tags and profiles are how he can tell someone is a real person.
- **Safety:** around age 10–12 he was targeted in Roblox by someone who named his cousin's street and described the house, then threatened them unless they handed over in-game items.
- **Awkward chats are lopsided:** "communication is held between a few people" and others are left out. Sketchy: someone asking for personal details.
- **Ideal hangout:** golf with 4 close buddies, "a very chill activity."

## Themes across all three interviews
- **Getting people to respond is the #1 pain point (3/3).** Read receipts with no reply, ghosting, and a few people taking over the conversation all make group chats feel awkward. Addie and Lanie brought this up without being asked about it directly.
- **Planning is hard, not interest (3/3).** All three had plans fall apart. The causes were clashing schedules, last-minute homework or shifts, and competing obligations. Everyone wants to hang out.
- **People join through someone they already know (3/3).** Roommates, a high school friend, a friend's boyfriend. Nobody joined a group cold.
- **The people decide whether a group is worth it (3/3).** Who's there matters more than what they're doing ("it's the people that make it").
- **Strangers online: comfort varies, the safety risk doesn't.** Comfort ranges from none (Lanie) to some (Addie) to daily (Anthony). But 2 of 3 described an unsafe or uncomfortable encounter they had as minors (harassment, doxxing threats).
- **They use apps they already have.** iMessage, Snapchat and GroupMe for chatting. Games either run inside the chat (Crazy Eights) or are console multiplayer. Nobody uses a dedicated planning app.
- **Casual, low-pressure games are popular.** Puzzle games come up a lot (Candy Crush, Block Blast, Sudoku), and Anthony and Addie both said they'd like to play them with friends.

## Design opportunities
- **Lightweight responses built into the chat:** one-tap yes/no/maybe and "find a time" polls in the chats people already use, so a reply takes no effort.
- **Gentle accountability:** a nudge to people who haven't answered, and confirmation as the event gets close, to cut down on no-shows.
- **Include quieter members:** make it easy for everyone to weigh in, not just the loudest few.
- **Short multiplayer versions of casual games played inside group chats,** as a reason to interact between hangouts.
- **Safety by default for anything with strangers:** a friends-of-friends model, no sharing personal info, clear signals that someone is a real person.
