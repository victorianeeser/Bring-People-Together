# 11/4 | Settle It user testing

I tested the Settle It idea with 5 people (ages 19 to 22). They reacted to the concept from my storyboard, where the group plays a quick Crazy Eights round in the chat and the winner picks dinner. Their feedback shaped the [prototype](README.md). Under each tester, **Prototype response** shows what changed because of what they said.

---

## Tester 1: Addie, 21
Addie loved the idea because her friends always struggle to decide where to eat. She thought turning the decision into a card game would make planning more fun and less frustrating.
- **What worked:** She understood right away that whoever wins gets to pick dinner.
- **What surprised me:** She was more excited about the competition than about the decision itself.
- **What flopped:** She wondered what happens if someone in the group doesn't want to play.
- **Prototype response:** Joining is optional. People tap **I'm In** on the game bubble, and only the people who join play. Leaning into the competition, Quick Draw shows everyone's reaction time after each round.

## Tester 2: Lanie, 20
Lanie thought Settle It was a fun way to avoid the endless back-and-forth in group chats. She liked that everyone gets an equal chance to make the final call.
- **What worked:** She understood how to start the game with the `/settle it` command.
- **What surprised me:** She suggested using it for other decisions, like picking movies or weekend plans.
- **Prototype response:** Added **Food, Movie, Plans, and Custom** categories.

## Tester 3: Anthony, 19
Anthony liked the idea of competing against his friends to decide where everyone goes. He thought it would make group chats more entertaining.
- **What worked:** He understood the winner's role right away and found the concept easy to follow.
- **What surprised me:** He suggested funny messages or animations when someone wins or loses.
- **What flopped:** He wasn't sure if it would open inside iMessage or need a separate app download.
- **Prototype response:** The result is "Sent with Confetti," the others see "You agreed to this" while the winner picks, and test friends react ("rigged", "ugh fine"). Settle It opens from the iMessage **+** drawer, like GamePigeon, so there's no separate app.

## Tester 4: Charles, 21
Charles thought the concept was creative because it takes something annoying, deciding where to eat, and turns it into a competition. He liked that the game makes the call so the group stops arguing.
- **What worked:** He liked how fast the game starts directly from the group chat.
- **What surprised me:** He suggested a shorter round for when everyone is in a hurry.
- **What flopped:** He pointed out that people might still disagree with the winner's pick, even after agreeing to play.
- **Prototype response:** Added a **10 / 15 / 30 second** join timer, and the starter can tap **Start Now**. The winner picks from options the group already put on the table, and the pick is posted to everyone as **SETTLED** with "No take-backs."

## Tester 5: Megan, 22
Megan thought Settle It would be useful because her friends constantly say they don't care where they eat, then reject every suggestion. She liked having a fun way to finally decide.
- **What worked:** She understood the purpose right away and could picture using it with her friends.
- **What surprised me:** She suggested letting everyone submit their preferred restaurants before the game starts.
- **What flopped:** She thought a card game might take too long for small decisions.
- **Prototype response:** Added **On the table**, which automatically lists the restaurants people mentioned in the chat, and anyone can add more before the game. Added **Lucky Draw**, where everyone flips one card and you're done in seconds.

---

## Patterns across testers
- **What worked:** All 5 understood "winner picks" right away. Nobody needed it explained.
- **What surprised me:** People cared more about the fun and the competition than about the decision. 3 of 5 asked for more: other decisions, win/lose animations, or submitting their own picks.
- **What flopped:** Time and buy-in. Two people worried a card game is too long. Two worried about people who won't play or won't accept the result.
- **What I changed:** Faster games (Quick Draw, Lucky Draw), adjustable join time, optional joining, categories, an "On the table" list from the chat, and a celebratory, no-take-backs result.
- **Still open:** Charles's point. A game can't force people to honor the result. The next test should be a real group dinner decision, to see if people actually go with the winner's pick.
